Upcoming Competition for THRILL

Written by Gracie Egner, Freshman, Mechanical Engineering

For our first competition since the formation of THRILL in Fall 2026, the group hopes to compete in the Ride Engineering Competition, hosted this year at Hersheypark in Hershey, Pennsylvania. The Ride Engineering Competition (REC) is a competition where students work in teams to design and develop a themed amusement ride. Teams combine engineering, creativity, and storytelling to create a ride experience similar to one at full scale.

THRILL has chosen to tackle this challenge by dividing the group into four main teams: mechanics, systems, structure, and theming.

Mechanics

The Mechanics Team will focus on the design of the roller coaster train, including systems such as the wheels, axles, and other mechanical components that allow the train to move safely along the track.

Systems

The Systems Team will be responsible for designing the roller coaster's braking system. This year, the REC requires that block zones be incorporated into the roller coaster.

Structure

The Structure Team will focus on the safety and structural support of the roller coaster. This includes ensuring that the ride can safely support the train and withstand the forces experienced during operation.

Theming

Lastly, the Theming Team will work on the ride's aesthetics and overall look. This team will create an immersive experience for riders by incorporating visual elements and storytelling into the ride. So far, the Theming Team has chosen a scary theme based on a Louisville legend: the Pope Lick Monster.

THRILL Member Spotlight

Written by Preston Koeninger, Freshman, Mechanical Engineering

My name is Preston Koeninger, and I am the Founder and President of THRILL. For years, I have been fascinated by how roller coasters work, and as that passion grew, I decided I wanted to make a career out of it. This led me to intensive research, connecting with industry professionals, and even touring The Gravity Group, a roller coaster design firm in Cincinnati. As college drew nearer, I knew engineering was the path that would give me the tools and knowledge to succeed in this industry, and the University of Louisville had the program to get me there.

When I arrived on campus, I noticed something was missing: a group dedicated to roller coaster engineering and the amusement industry. The more flyers I hung up and the more conversations I had, the more people I met who would enjoy and find benefit in a group like THRILL. While THRILL is still young, I see enormous potential, not only in the group itself, but more importantly in the individual members.

In our first month, THRILL has already accomplished more than I could have ever imagined. We have registered a team for the national Ride Engineering Competition next spring, lined up multiple guest speakers with more to come, planned a facility tour at Kentucky Kingdom, and now officially published our first issue of Industry Inversion!

Louisville Legend: What Is the Pope Lick Monster?

Written by Gracie Egner, Freshman, Mechanical Engineering

The Pope Lick Monster is a creature from Louisville, Kentucky, said to live near the Pope Lick railway trestle. According to local legend, the monster is a goat-like creature with human features. Stories claim that it uses hypnosis to lure people onto the railroad tracks so that they cannot escape. Although there is no evidence that the monster actually exists, the legend has become a well-known part of Louisville's history and local folklore.

The legend is believed to have originated in the early 20th century. Over time, stories about the creature spread throughout the Louisville area, creating several different versions of its origins and abilities. The combination of the monster's unusual appearance, mysterious behavior, and the real dangers surrounding the railroad trestle has helped the story remain popular. Today, the Pope Lick Monster is one of the more recognizable legends associated with Louisville.

This Louisville legend is THRILL's main inspiration for this year's Ride Engineering Competition (REC). In addition to a working model roller coaster, THRILL is working to create an immersive atmosphere, inspired by Disney's use of environmental storytelling within their parks. The team in charge of this feat, THRILL's Theming Team, has chosen to weave the legend of the Pope Lick Monster into the ride using goat motifs, along with a Pope Lick Monster figure at the end.

The team has already begun developing concept art and ideas for decorations, props, and elements that will bring the legend to life throughout the ride. By incorporating this into the environment, the THRILL Theming Team hopes to create an experience that feels completely immersive.

This experience through THRILL is a very valuable one. Not only does this theme give THRILL's Theming Team firsthand experience working on the theming of an attraction, but it also allows them to pay homage to the city they call home and draw so much inspiration from. By combining hands-on experience with a piece of Louisville folklore, the team is able to display their city with pride in an unexpected way: with the help of a local legend.

Your Home Park: A Conversation with Kentucky Kingdom's James Atkinson II

Interview by Vice President AJ Wyckoff and Secretary Micah Shively · Edited by President Preston Koeninger

James Atkinson II grew up in Louisville and calls Kentucky Kingdom his home park. He started there as a ride operator in 2023. Now in his fourth season, he's the park's Marketing and Sales Coordinator, and he's a lifetime passholder. Atkinson spoke to THRILL members at the Speed School's ESSRB building earlier this semester. This fall, AJ and Micah caught up with him at the park during Pumpkins at Kentucky Kingdom to talk about the fall lineup, his favorite coaster, and why UofL students should make the short drive over.

This interview has been lightly edited for length and clarity. The full interview can be viewed on THRILL's Instagram, @thrill.uofl.

ShivelyWhat does the fall season have to offer at Kentucky Kingdom?

AtkinsonThe fall season brings Pumpkins at Kentucky Kingdom. This is our fifth year having it. When Herschend Family Entertainment purchased the park in 2021, they really wanted more family events, so Pumpkins came in 2022 and Christmas at Kentucky Kingdom followed in 2024. This is also the first year we're home to the original Jack O'Lantern Spectacular, and it's included with admission. You don't pay extra. Twenty-five phenomenally talented local carvers carve over 5,000 pumpkins by hand. We drained one of our two lazy rivers, which is a little more than a quarter mile long, and after dusk every night we're open, you walk the pumpkin trail around it and take it all in.

All the rides are included too. You can ride Wind Chaser, Lightning Run, and the new Flying Fox at night. We have great food. My favorite is probably the pumpkin spice deep fried Oreos, and as you can tell, I've had enough of those already. Something new this year is a costume parade, where the little kids get to parade around the park. That's what we have to offer, and there's a lot more I could go on and on about.

WyckoffYou mentioned some of the rides Kentucky Kingdom has to offer. What's your favorite?

AtkinsonThis is an easy answer for me. My first title here was ride operator on Wind Chaser in 2023, and it's my favorite coaster. I'd have to check my log, but I hit 2,726 lifetime rides on it during Louder Than Life, which we're also open for. That's my ride. Every twisting corner of it. I operated it, and now I help people film on it and talk about it. It's one of the smaller RMCs, but as a wise man once said, a small RMC is still an elite ride.

"A small RMC is still an elite ride."

ShivelyWithout saying too much, what is one reason to get excited about the upcoming season?

AtkinsonThis upcoming season has a lot going on. Pumpkins at Kentucky Kingdom featuring the original Jack O'Lantern Spectacular is continuing. Kentucky Kingdom and Hurricane Bay are both on the same admission and open at the same time. Christmas at Kentucky Kingdom is coming up. There's so much to look forward to, and you can bet on it being a pretty great season next year at Kentucky Kingdom.

WyckoffWhy should UofL students pay Kentucky Kingdom a visit, or maybe even buy a Gold Pass?

AtkinsonIf you're at Louisville, or you're part of THRILL at UofL, you should really pay us a visit. We're your home park, and we're here for you. I was on campus about a month ago handing out free tickets to students at the Student Activities Center. The Kingdom has your back. I just graduated from college, so I know midterms and finals are tough. The best thing that got me through them was roller coasters, flat rides, and being around thousands of people who are all here for a common goal: creating memories worth repeating.

It's maybe a five to seven minute drive from campus, maybe even less. Every season pass, whether it's Gold, Diamond, or Silver, comes with free parking and unlimited admission from the start of May all the way through the first week of January.
